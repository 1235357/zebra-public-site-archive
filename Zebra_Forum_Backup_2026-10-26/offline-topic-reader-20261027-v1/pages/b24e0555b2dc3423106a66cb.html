                  Hello, I'm designing an internal company product that utilizes a microcontroller interface that prints to a ZD621 printer and takes in input from an RS232 stream from industrial equipment and is controlled by a bluetooth app. I'm currently powering the microcontroller from the printer's USB-A port, however, it would solve so many design problems if I could power it from the 5V rail on pin 1 or pin 9 from the printers RS232 DB9 port. However every time I try to add any kind of load between pin 9 and pin 5, the printer's onboard FET collapses the voltage down to .1V and then cycles on and off. I can get SOME improvement by using a 'heavy duty' scanner cable which seems to have something built in that pumps the voltage to 5.5v. However once again, when i add a device in between 9 and 5, the rail collapses to 1.6v. What kind of power conditioning is required to safely keep a steady 5V from the port? I would like to remove all reliance on USB and just design the rs232 connector into the PCB so I can just have one cable end going to the industrial equipment. ANY HELP AT ALL or pointing in the right direction would make me eternally grateful.

            For my application (scanner w/ cradle to ZD621 via RS232 simultaneously with USB Keyboard attached), I needed to use a cable with a separate power supply (and gender changing adapter). Without this configuration, the power was cut from USB during cradle charging.This doc might help you find the needed cables and/or power adapters: https://www.zebra.com/content/dam/zebra_dam/en/guide/configuration-and-accessories/barcode-scanners-cable-guide-accessory-en-us.xlsx

            Thank you. I am familiar with the serial cables that support external power, however this is exactly the thing I'm trying to distance my solution from. I'm trying to eliminate extra power cables all together. The microcontroller should not be using more than 600 milliamps of power at peak transmission, Zebra's own documentation says there is a 750 mA power budget shared between the serial port and the USB-A port (hence why your solution would brown out, using a scanner/cradle combo plus a keyboard is probably pulling too much). All the technical documentation I have says the serial port alone should be able to provide enough current and I've even tried using Zebra's own product cables to power my device. However, if I even try to power an LED on Pin 9/5 (literally only an LED, Pin 9 - LED, Resistor - Pin 5) the printers' built in FET protection kicks in and won't provide the power to any load I put on it. This is reproduceable across ZF, GX, and GK series printers. I think there's something missing from the equation.What I think is missing is some kind of hardware solution to provide power conditioning to the load to slowly ramp up the current draw rather than pulling all at once. I've ordered some additional microchips to try and test this while I wait for a response from Zebra's product design team. The other possibility is that there is some signal required on one of the other seldom used DB9 pins (ready to receive / ready to send pins maybe?) but at the moment I don't have an easy way to listen on every pin to find out what that might be.
